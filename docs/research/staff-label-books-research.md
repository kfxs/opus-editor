# Staff labels — what the engraving books say

> **Findings only**, in the manner of `docs/research/glissando-books-research.md`. ⛔ Nothing here is a
> decision; the choices are made in `docs/plans/staff-label-plan.md`, and this file is one of its lists
> of options. ⚠️ A research file does not know what was DECIDED.
>
> Written 2026-09-28 from the library on disk (`reference/README.md`): **Gould, *Behind Bars*** (the
> whole book; pp. 507–510 and Table 2 on p. 509 read on the **scans** at 400 dpi, the rest from the
> OCR), **Ross**, **Stone** and **Gerou & Lusk** (their text layers). Pages marked *(OCR)* were found in
> the OCR and not re-read on the scan — re-read them there before quoting them as settled.
> Read, Powell and the full Sibelius book are not on disk: **UNKNOWN**, which does NOT mean silent.
>
> The question: the text before a staff at the start of each system — the instrument name, and every
> label that is NOT one (voices, roles, players, sketches).

## 0. Synthesis

- ⭐ **The books already label staves with things that are not instruments** — voice parts, characters,
  *solo* / *tutti*, *Primo* / *Secondo*, *Manual* / *Pedal*, percussion PLAYER numbers, choir groups.
  A label that is not an instrument name is ordinary practice; only a SKETCH's or a LAYER's label is
  found in no book (§6).
- **Full name on the first system, abbreviated after** — Gould, Ross and Gerou & Lusk agree (§2).
- **A small ensemble that keeps every staff on every system needs no labels after the first page**
  (Gould p. 507) — the book's own case for "first system only".
- **A label change shows on a SUBSEQUENT system**; mid-system, the change is written at the music (§2).
- **One label for a braced instrument, centred on the brace** (Table 2) — a label may belong to a GROUP (§4).
- **The first system is INDENTED** — Gould and Stone state it; **Gerou & Lusk give the number, ½ inch** (§1).
- **Three alignments**, all Gould's: centred in the margin (traditional), right-justified, left-justified (§1).
- House-style NUMBERS: the ½-inch indent (G&L), the gap to the system (**2.7–2.8 sp** from the barline,
  MEASURED on Gould's Table 2, never stated), the size (never stated; measured ≈ 0.85 × her tempo text).

## 1. Placement

- **Horizontal — three styles, Gould p. 508 (scan):** *"Instrument labels may be centred in the margin
  space – this is the most traditional layout (see Table 2, column (a)). Alternatively, they may be
  justified right (b) or even justified left (c)."* With centred or left-justified labels, *"move the
  numerals to the right, up to the margin brackets, so that they are close to their staves."*
- **Vertical — DRAWN, not stated (Table 2, p. 509, scan):** each label is centred on its own staff's
  middle line (Timpani: the lower-case centre measured 0.1 sp off it). A braced instrument (Harp, Organ)
  takes ONE label centred on the brace — for the Organ the brace covers the two manual staves only and
  the pedal staff has no label. "Percussion" with player numerals 1 and 2 is centred on its bracketed pair.
- **Gap to the system — a NUMBER, measured only:** in column (b) the right edge of "Timpani" stands
  ≈ **2.9 sp** from the systemic barline (23 px at a staff space of ≈ 8 px, 400 dpi). No book states a gap.
  ⭐ **Re-measured 2026-09-28 at 600 dpi (1 sp = 11.9 px):** every label's right edge in column (b) sits on
  ONE column whether or not a bracket is present — Piccolo 1795, Flutes 1793, Tuba 1794, Timpani 1793 (no
  bracket), Harp 1795, Cello 1798 — **2.7–2.8 sp from the systemic barline**, **1.8–2.0 sp from the bracket's
  outer edge** where there is one. ⇒ the column is set against the BARLINE, with the bracket's room kept free,
  ⛔ not measured to each bracket. Stone p. 170: "Picc." ends **1.6 sp** before the bracket.
- **Indentation:**
  - Gould p. 507 (scan): *"The first system of each movement should be indented – this provides margin
    space for labelling."* Gould p. 486 *(OCR)*: movements are indented *"even when instrument names do
    not need to be repeated."*
  - Stone, §A "Opening Measures" (≈ p. 46): *"always indent the first line."*
  - ⭐ **The amount — Gerou & Lusk p. 136** (Staff) **and p. 140** (Systems): *"The first system of music
    is usually indented about ½ inch."* (`reference/README.md` said no source gave it; corrected 2026-09-28.)
- **Width, Gould p. 510 (scan):** *"The longest abbreviation determines the size of the margin… keep
  abbreviations short."* ⇒ the margin on later systems follows the widest SHORT label.

## 2. Full vs abbreviated, omission, change

- **Gould p. 507 (scan):** *"On the opening page of music, label each stave with the unabbreviated
  instrument or voice name in the margin."* Full labels are repeated at each movement. Orchestral and
  large scores: *"all subsequent pages take … labelling in the margin, abbreviated."* ⭐ *"A small
  ensemble score that retains all staves on each system throughout … needs no further margin labelling
  after the first page."*
- **Agreement:** Ross p. 242 — names in full on the first system, abbreviated afterwards; Gerou & Lusk
  p. 117 the same.
- ⚠️ **Conflict on plurals:** Gould p. 510, *"Plurals are unnecessary."* — Ross p. 242, *"The use of the
  plural of these abbreviations is common."*
- **Abbreviations (Gould p. 510):** capitalise every first letter ("C.A."); full stops are common practice;
  avoid ambiguous ones (*cor*).
- **A change of instrument:**
  - Gould p. 510: mark it at the first available rest, *"Thereafter change the instrument label in the
    margin of a subsequent system."* The "to Piccolo / to Flute" example is on p. 507.
  - Gould p. 513 *(OCR)*, a keyboard doubler: *"at an instrument change that occurs mid-system, centre a
    label between the two staves."*
  - Parts: Gould p. 559 *(OCR)* — "take …", "Change to …", "to …", made to stand out; Ross p. 206 puts the
    new name, usually abbreviated, above the bar where it starts.
- **Doublings:** Gould p. 507 (scan), they *"may be given in brackets"* in the opening label, though an
  instrumentation list is more usual.
- **Solo piano / single-staff scores: UNKNOWN** — no sentence found. In a PART the name goes above or
  below the title, flush left (Gould p. 558 *(OCR)*; Ross p. 218, *"never aligned with the indented first staff"*).

## 3. Typeface and size

- Gould p. 508 (scan): the typeface *"should match the one chosen for text instructions within the score."*
- Table 2 draws the labels in **roman**, not italic.
- **Point size: UNKNOWN** — no source STATES one. ⭐ Measured 2026-09-28: in Table 2 the label's cap height
  is ≈ **0.85 ×** the bold tempo "Allegro" (22 px against 26 px; x-height ≈ 1.3 sp, cap height 1.85 sp — but
  on a reduced staff, so the RATIO to the tempo text is the number that transfers). Stone p. 170: "Picc." cap
  height = the bar number *20*'s digit height, both **≈ 1.5 sp**.

## 4. One label for a group of staves

- A brace shares one label (Harp, Organ, Percussion 1/2 in Table 2).
- Separate choirs: *"label each group in the margin: Group/Choir I/A, II/B"* (Gould p. 463 *(OCR)*).
- *"Centre margin labels soli/soloists and Chorus on the relevant groups of staves"* (p. 463 *(OCR)*). In
  Table 2, CHORUS is set VERTICALLY (rotated) in columns (b) and (c), left of S/A/T/B; horizontal in (a).
- Off-stage staves: a thin vertical bracket *"to the left of the instrument labels"* carrying the
  description; likewise a conductor or group label (Gould p. 513 *(OCR)*).
- Divisi: Table 2 labels staves "altri div." and "Viola div."; div. conventions on p. 428.

## 5. Numbers, several lines, transposition (Gould pp. 508, 510, scan)

- Arabic numerals for individual players; roman for string sections ("Violin I" / "Violins I").
- Traditional "2 Flutes"; modern "Flutes 1.2". A full point separates numerals *only on the same line*;
  numerals may run left to right or be stacked.
- Label by key, *"Clarinet in B♭"*; "in C" only for genuine C instruments.
- *"Abbreviations of instruments defined by key may be split onto two lines"* ("Cl. 1 / in B♭", Table 2 (c))
  ⇒ a label may be MULTI-LINE and may contain an accidental.

## 6. Labels that are not instrument names

- **Voices** (Gould p. 462 *(OCR)*): in the indented opening margin, usually in full; unnecessary later
  *"when the layout remains the same"*, and if it changes, label every staff. Divided parts S1/S2 (also p. 468).
- **Solo / tutti** (p. 463 *(OCR)*): *"Mark a solo line solo… label the stave tutti"*, possibly in capitals.
- **Characters** (≈ p. 470 *(OCR)*): the name in full in small capitals above and left of the first entry;
  on later systems *"label the character in the margin, abbreviated if necessary."*
- **Players:** piano duet Primo/Secondo or I/II, Piano I/II (p. 330 *(OCR)*); organ Manual/Man. and
  Pedal/Ped., *"not strictly necessary"* for three staves (p. 343 *(OCR)*); percussion staves by player
  number (p. 272 *(OCR)*).
- **Percussion:** label the staff "Percussion" and name each instrument above its entry; the instrument's
  name in the margin only when one player plays one instrument throughout (p. 508, scan). Where they
  alternate often, list them all at the start of each system (p. 275 *(OCR)*). Stone (≈ pp. 205–207, not
  checked on the scan): *"Instrument specifications may be placed in the left-hand margins or above the
  pertinent staff-line(s)."*
- **Left / right hand:** beside the stem, ⛔ not in the margin (Gould ≈ p. 300 *(OCR)*, *"label the
  crossing hand beside the first stem"*).
- **A sketch's or a layer's label ("Sketch A", "Idea 2"): UNKNOWN** — found in none of the four books.

## 7. Structural rules vs NUMBERS

- **Structural:** full first / abbreviated later · three alignments · centred on the staff, or on the
  braced/bracketed group · first page only for a small score that never changes layout · relabel on the
  next system after a change · rotated group labels (drawn only) · one label for a braced instrument.
- **Numbers (a house style's defaults, rows):** the ½-inch indent (G&L) · the ≈ 2.9 sp gap (measured) ·
  the point size (**UNKNOWN**).
