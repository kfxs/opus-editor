> **Gerou & Lusk**. Pages marked **(scan)** were read on the rendered page; every MEASUREMENT is off a scan

## 0. Synthesis

- ⭐ **Gould p. 484 is his rule, word for word:** *"Place bar numbers at the beginning of each system, ideally
  above the clef of the top stave."* — and *"The first bar of a piece (or movement) is not labelled."*
- ⚠️ **Stone disagrees on frequency:** every FIFTH bar, above its barline, is *"the customary method"*
  (p. 168); system-start numbering is *"another method often encountered"* (p. 169).
- **A pickup is not counted** — *"The first complete bar (and not an up-beat) is bar 1"* (Gould p. 484);
  *"upbeat measures are not counted"* (Stone p. 168).
- **Italic, unframed** — to tell them from roman page numbers and from framed rehearsal marks (Gould p. 484).
- **Measured on the plates:** digit height **1.4–1.65 sp**, baseline **2.0–2.4 sp** above the top line, a
  **≈ 0.5 sp** white gap to the clef, the left ink **flush with the staff's left end** (Gould p. 490).
- **Point size: UNKNOWN** — no book states one.

## 1. Where the numbers go

- **Gould p. 484 (scan):** *"Place bar numbers at the beginning of each system, ideally above the clef of the
  top stave. To number every five or ten bars gives these bars an apparent significance that they do not
  have, and should be avoided."*
- ⚠️ **Stone p. 168 (scan):** *"The customary method is to mark every fifth measure by placing a numeral above
  the barline at the beginning of the measure to be designated."* Stone p. 169 §3, system-start numbering as
  *"another method often encountered"*: in scores *"just right or left above the first top clef of each brace
  (though not on the opening measure of the movement)"*; in parts *"above the clefs or in front of each line,
  i.e., in the left margin"* (p. 170 draws both).
- **The first bar is not numbered:** Gould p. 484 (above); Stone p. 169 (*"not on the opening measure of the movement"*).
- **The pickup:** neither book writes "bar 0"; both say it is not counted — Gould p. 484, *"The first complete
  bar (and not an up-beat) is bar 1."*; Stone p. 168, *"upbeat measures are not counted"*.
- **When NOT to use bar numbers:** where performers have different numbers of bars, or barlines do not
  coincide — use rehearsal marks (Gould p. 484).

## 2. Which staves carry them

- **Gould p. 484:** *"the top stave"* only.
- **Stone p. 169 (scan):** orchestra *"above the score and above the strings"*; band above the score and above
  the brass; vocal above the voices and above the piano; choral above the voices and above the accompaniment.
- Below the bottom staff in large scores: **UNKNOWN**.

## 3. Style

- **Gould p. 484 (scan):** *"It is best to use italic to differentiate bar numbers from roman-type page
  numbers"*; they *"should not be framed in boxes or circles where rehearsal figures are also present."*
- **Rehearsal marks, by contrast (Gould pp. 484–485, scan):** *"conspicuous non-italic bold type"*, often the
  bold time-signature face, boxed or circled, over the system, centred on the barline or flush-left with it.
- **Stone p. 169 (scan):** if bar numbers are framed, *"a square or rectangular frame is more practical than a
  circle"*; in parts boxed numbers go *"above the barline or slightly to the right"* (p. 168).
- **G&L p. 117 (scan):** rehearsal numbers *"may be letters or measure numbers enclosed in a circle or square"*.
- **Size in points: UNKNOWN.**

## 4. Numbering conventions

- **1st / 2nd endings, Gould p. 237 (scan) — two options, both drawn:** duplicated *19a 20a 21a | 19b 20b 21b*
  ("some editions"; the second-time bracket must then enclose all the duplicated bars) · or, preferred for
  ensembles, *"number consecutively through separate endings"*. **Stone p. 168:** *"simply count all measures,
  regardless of repeat signs"*.
- **Repeated sections, Gould p. 240 (scan):** numbering *"should run consecutively through all written sections
  … A section involving skipping a passage (e.g. to a coda) will have a corresponding skip in bar numbers."*
- **Written-out repeats, Stone p. 169:** when score and parts repeat differently, use rehearsal marks, reusing
  one *"followed by a small 'a'"*.
- **A bar split across systems, Gould p. 490 (scan):** *"Place the bar number on the new system in
  parenthesis"* — drawn italic *121* then *(122)*.
- **Multi-bar rests:** Stone p. 169 — with 5- or 10-bar numbering a multirest may not be longer than 5 or 10
  bars; system-start numbering *"permits longer multirests"*. Gould pp. 564–565 *(OCR)*: the count in bold,
  the rest divided at rehearsal marks and tempo / meter changes; p. 565 *(OCR)*, *"A single bar's rest is best
  numbered as '1'."* A range label ("12–15"): **UNKNOWN**.
- **Restart per movement:** only implied (Gould's "piece (or movement)", Stone's "opening measure of the movement").
- **Inserted "23a" bars in editions:** **UNKNOWN** beyond the two cases above.
- **Parts match the score:** Gould p. 519 *(OCR)*, Stone p. 169.

## 5. Collisions

- **Stone p. 169 (scan):** *"If a measure number coincides with a tempo indication or other instruction
  appearing above the staff, it is placed above such instructions."*
- ⚠️ **Possible conflict, Gould p. 520 (scan):** *"It is only ever rehearsal marks and first- and second-time
  ending indications that go above tempi."* Read literally that puts bar numbers BELOW tempi — an inference;
  she does not address bar numbers there.
- With the clef or notes above the staff: no stated rule; only the drawn plates (§6).

## 7. Where the books disagree

1. **Every system vs every 5th bar** — Gould (system; every 5/10 "should be avoided") vs Stone ("customary").
2. **Above or below a tempo mark** — Stone above; Gould reserves the place above tempi for rehearsal marks
   and endings (inference).
3. **Duplicated a/b numbering in endings** — Gould allows it as one option; Stone and Gould's own preference
   count straight through.

**Not checked:** Ross on bar numbers — nothing in his text layer for "measure number" / "bar number" /
numbering, but not read page by page: **UNKNOWN**, not silent.
